A measurable water proxy

NaCl concentration changes are calibrated; pollutant identity remains unresolved

At 1550 nm: n(W) = −0.0800 W³ + 0.0740 W² + 0.1620 W + 1.3162

W is salt mass fraction. Around 500 mg/kg, dn/dc = 1.621 × 10⁻⁷ RIU per mg/kg.

Proposed 10 µm interaction length gives 6.570 × 10⁻⁶ rad per mg/kg.

YSI EXO field reference: salinity accuracy ±1% or 0.1 ppt; temperature accuracy ±0.01 °C.

Field accuracy bounds are not shot-noise standard deviations. This is a change detector, not a safety test.

A credible next experiment

Validate a four-tap optical prototype before extrapolating to a regional network

Calibrate NaCl phase response with standards and controlled temperature at 1550 nm.

Measure noise spectra, sample loss, modulation efficiency and squeezing at the same optical dose.

Compare coherent, independent squeezed and distributed readout using blind injected changes.

Validate drift, fouling and false alarms over hours; measure actual optical and electrical resources.

Advance only if total error improves. Keep chemical identification and spatial coverage as separate requirements.

Assumptions that control the result

These are design targets, not measured specifications of a quantum water detector

10 nW/node; 10 µm effective optical interaction; 500 mg/kg background; 1-second decisions.

10 dB maximum source squeezing; 1 MHz effective mode rate; 10 mrad phase-lock RMS.

95% detector, 90% sample, 90% coupling; 0.05 dB splitter excess/stage.

100 m pilot links; regional links = 1.3× geographic distance; 0.18 dB/km fiber anchor.

0.05 mK compensated common residual; 60 s correlation; 0.03 mg/kg independent calibration RMS.

10 mK field stress test; 1 nW–70 µW power sweep. Full register: docs/SOURCES.md.
